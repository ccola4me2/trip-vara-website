Quoting and booking

From the first question to the deposit.

Most of this is retyping that somebody else already typed. The portal reads it instead.

  • Two or three choices on a quote

    An inside, an oceanview and a balcony on one quote. The portal keeps what you offered as well as what they took.

  • Proposals out

    Every quote still unanswered, split by the only thing that matters: waiting on the client, or waiting on you.

  • Read the confirmation, do not retype it

    Paste the vendor confirmation and the booking number, ship, dates, cabin, guests and money come off it.

  • Group proposals, read off the PDF

    Norwegian, Celebrity, Margaritaville and a general reader pull out the dates, the hold, the tariff and the amenities.

  • Group space

    A page that takes names for a departure, cabins counted against what is held, and every enquiry landing on the group.

  • Cruise search

    Every sailing in the catalog, searched inside the portal instead of in four supplier sites.

  • Specials, with a death date

    What a BDM sent you, what you negotiated, the group rate with three cabins left, and which of them dies on Friday.

  • What cancelling costs today

    The penalty tiers off the vendor confirmation, counted against today, so you can answer the question every client asks.

  • Fill in the gaps

    The reservations missing a confirmation number or a date that the reminders and the commission both depend on.

  • Sixteen kinds of thing to sell

    Cruise, hotel, tour, air, rail, insurance, a visa, a car park. Each priced the way its own vendor pays.

The money

Nothing here is a single field with a tickbox.

Commission was one number and a status. That is why underpayments were invisible and lapsed credits were nobody's job.

  • Every charge knows if it earns

    Cruise fare earns. Port taxes, government fees and non-commissionable fare do not. Gratuities and packages depend on the vendor.

  • Payments due

    Deposit, part payments and the final balance, on the vendor's real deadline with a reminder a week in front of it.

  • Commission owed, and aged

    Who owes you, how much, and how long it has been owed. Three questions an owner asks and none of them used to have an answer.

  • What the vendor actually paid

    Reconciled against what was expected, so a short payment, a held bonus or a netted-off chargeback shows up as a figure.

  • Splits

    The advisor's standing agreement, or a different share frozen onto one reservation. Set by the agency, never by the advisor.

  • Payouts

    What the agency actually paid each advisor, and when. The last step of the money, on the record instead of in a memory.

  • Credits with an expiry

    Future cruise deposits, credits and goodwill certificates, with the date they stop being worth anything. The vendor will not ring.

  • TC and bonus commission

    Kept in full by the advisor who earned it, with casino deals split like anything else.

  • Casino and comped sailings

    A client paying only taxes and fees is not a broken reservation. The commission is still there and still split.

Your book of business

Including the part of it you are not working.

A list of six hundred clients tells you nothing. The useful question is which twelve of them to ring this week.

  • Clients

    One record per person: their birthday, their passport and its expiry, their trips, and where they came from.

  • Households

    The fact that two of those rows are married. Put one on a reservation and the rest of the house is offered as travellers.

  • Two records, one person

    Robert Smith and Bob Smith merged into one, with everything each of them was carrying kept.

  • Who to call, every Monday

    Five lists off records you already keep: gone quiet, money lapsing, just home, birthdays, anniversaries. Emailed, so it needs no remembering.

  • Leads

    Everybody who is not a client yet, each with a next step and a date that puts them on your calendar.

  • Sales opportunities

    Where every live reservation stands, as a board you work across rather than a column you sort.

  • Reviews and referrals

    The portal already knew who came home and has not been rung. Now it asks them, and records who they sent you.

  • Where clients come from

    Ten channels, one origin per client, inherited by their reservations. No free text, so Facebook and FB stop being two channels.

  • Bring your book across

    Select the rows in whatever you use now and paste them in. Clients and reservations both, because no back office gives you a clean export.

The working day

What is waiting, where you cannot walk past it.

Everything that needs somebody happens on a day. Until this, each kind was only visible on the screen that owned it.

  • A dashboard that says what it could not build

    Panels you pin and reorder, and a named failure instead of a blank space when one of them cannot be worked out.

  • The month, with everything on it

    Appointments, tasks, a lead's next step, departures, returns and vendor deadlines. Every reason Thursday is not free.

  • To do

    The day's work from every corner of the portal in one list, not five.

  • Task templates

    The four things that follow every cruise, written once and hung off a date on the reservation so they land when they matter.

  • The bell

    The same list as the morning email, in the portal, for the people who do not read email at eight.

  • Your phone, with the tab shut

    A push notification that deliberately carries nothing. It knocks, and the portal is asked what is waiting.

  • Chat, advisors only

    No route by which a client reaches any of it. That absence is why you can write "the deposit is wobbling" next to the booking it is about.

  • One search field

    Clients, reservations, vendors and leads, grouped. A forward slash from anywhere puts the cursor in it.

  • Targets with a pace

    A year's number, and whether today puts you ahead of where you should be or behind it.

What the client sees

What you chose to share, and nothing you did not.

Their half of this is a page they can look at, which is the difference between answering a question once and answering it every three weeks.

  • One trip, on a link

    What is booked, what they have paid, what falls due next, and the documents you chose to share.

  • Everything you hold for them

    Eight reservations that are one holiday in Spain, answered as one page in the order it will happen.

  • The itinerary, day by day

    The flight at 6:40, the transfer that meets it, which night is the captain's dinner, and the afternoon they should be told is free.

  • An itinerary library

    The Palancar Reef snorkelling described once, with the meeting time right, reused instead of retyped in slightly different words.

  • They can sign in

    A link cannot tell two people apart. An account can, and taking it off one person leaves the rest of the household alone.

  • Documents

    The confirmation, the insurance policy, the air tickets, the visa letter. Held once, so "can you send that again" is a link.

  • A statement they can read

    Everything the reservation knows, minus the commission rate, the splits, the passport numbers, the card digits and your own notes.

  • Your own forms

    Built here, hosted here, and submitted into your own database as a client rather than as an email you have to retype.

  • QR codes

    A square somebody can point a phone at, for a form, a group page or a special, on anything you hand out.

  • Automations

    A sequence that can wait days between steps, because where it got to is recorded rather than held in memory.

The agency

More than one advisor, and more than one agency.

Harmless with one advisor, the whole thing with six. Who can see what is decided in one place rather than screen by screen.

  • Your agency, fenced

    Every read and every write is bounded by the agency the person is in. Nothing widens by accident, because widening is not the default.

  • An advisor sees their own commission

    Their own and nobody else's, including what the agency keeps. Guarded by tests, not by everyone remembering.

  • Advisors

    Add them, suspend them, set what each one keeps. The owner can do it, and only inside their own agency.

  • Your name on the front of it

    Your agency name, your mark, your colour and your tagline on every page a client of yours opens.

  • One vendor directory

    Rates, desk contacts, booking instructions, payment terms and the supplier's own website at the top of the record. Nobody rebuilds it from memory.

  • Production reports

    What was sold, by whom, by month and by vendor, out of the book itself rather than a copy of it.

  • Training

    The whole portal in the order somebody would learn it, not nine lessons that stop at a first booking.

  • A manual and a cheat sheet

    One for the advisor, one for whoever runs the agency, both written for the things a screen cannot tell you.

  • Join by link

    Send your advisors one address. They sign up against your agency, and you approve them before they see anything.
